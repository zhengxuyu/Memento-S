# A Truly Nefarious Neurosurgeon

Imagine a neurosurgeon secretly rewires your brain so you want to do exactly what you would have chosen anyway. Are you less free? This probes whether what matters for autonomy is the causal history of a decision or its current functional properties.

## When to Apply
- Debating whether an agent's trained behavior is "really its own"
- Evaluating if fine-tuning or prompt engineering undermines genuine agency
- An argument about autonomy focuses on causal origins rather than current competence

## Procedure
1. Identify the behavior whose autonomy is questioned
2. Imagine the same behavior was implanted by an external manipulator
3. Ask: does the causal history change your evaluation, or only the current functional state?
4. Decide which criterion (history vs. function) matters for your actual purpose

## Example
An RL agent develops a cautious exploration strategy through reward shaping. Critics say "it was forced to be cautious." The nefarious neurosurgeon test: if the agent would have converged on caution anyway, does the reward shaping undermine the strategy's validity?

## Anti-Pattern
Don't use this to excuse any manipulation as harmless. The point is to clarify what specifically bothers us about external influence, not to dismiss all concerns.
