# The Curse of the Cauliflower

You can't describe the taste of cauliflower to someone who's never tasted it. This ineffability is real but mundane — it's a limitation of language, not evidence of a supernatural flavor-property. Many experiences resist verbal description. That's interesting, but it doesn't make them mysterious or non-physical.

## When to Apply
- Someone argues that because X can't be fully described, X must be non-physical
- Ineffability is treated as evidence of deep metaphysical mystery
- You're stuck because an experience or observation resists clean verbalization

## Procedure
1. Identify the claim: "X is indescribable, therefore X is special/non-physical"
2. Ask: is the indescribability a feature of X, or a limitation of the description method?
3. Consider whether other mundane things are equally hard to describe
4. Separate genuine ineffability (a communication problem) from mystery (an ontological claim)
5. Look for alternative ways to convey the information (demonstration, comparison, analogy)

## Example
An agent encounters a game mechanic it can't articulate in its hypothesis language. This doesn't mean the mechanic is incomprehensible — it means the description framework needs enriching. The "ineffability" is in the vocabulary, not the phenomenon.

## Anti-Pattern
Don't dismiss genuine communication challenges. Some things really are hard to put into words — just don't elevate that difficulty into a metaphysical puzzle.
