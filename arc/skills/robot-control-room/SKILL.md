# Trapped in the Robot Control Room

Imagine being inside a robot, watching screens and pressing buttons. Who watches the screens? This thought experiment reveals the Cartesian Theater fallacy: the assumption that perception requires an internal observer viewing internal representations. This creates an infinite regress — the inner observer needs its own observer.

## When to Apply
- An explanation of perception or cognition implicitly requires an inner observer
- Someone describes a system as "seeing" or "reading" its own internal states
- A model has a central bottleneck where "everything comes together" for interpretation

## Procedure
1. Identify where the explanation assumes something is "presented" to an inner agent
2. Ask: who or what is the audience for this internal presentation?
3. If the answer requires another agent, you have a homunculus problem
4. Redesign: replace the inner observer with distributed processes that act directly
5. Verify the new explanation doesn't reintroduce a hidden observer

## Example
"The visual cortex creates a 3D model, and then the mind inspects it." Who inspects it? If another module, what inspects that module's output? Instead: the visual cortex produces signals that directly influence motor planning and decision-making — no central theater needed.

## Anti-Pattern
Don't confuse useful architectural diagrams (which may show data flowing to a module) with claims about inner observers. The fallacy is specifically about assuming conscious observation at some internal stage.
