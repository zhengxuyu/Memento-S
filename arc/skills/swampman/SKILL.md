# Swampman Meets a Cow-Shark

Davidson's Swampman: lightning creates a molecule-for-molecule duplicate of you from swamp matter. It has no history, no evolutionary past, no learning. Is it you? Does it have beliefs? Dennett pairs this with a "cow-shark" — an object that looks and acts exactly like a cow but descended from sharks. These cases test whether identity and function require history.

## When to Apply
- Debating whether two structurally identical things are functionally identical
- Testing whether your theory of X (meaning, function, identity) relies on history
- Intuition pumps about personal identity, teleportation, or AI consciousness

## Procedure
1. Construct the thought experiment: same structure, different history (or no history)
2. Apply your theory: does it say the duplicate has property X?
3. Check your intuitions: do they align with your theory?
4. If intuitions conflict with theory, examine which should yield
5. Use the conflict to refine your understanding of what history contributes

## Example
If Swampman acts exactly like you in every way, most practical purposes treat it as you. But if your theory of meaning requires causal history (your word "water" means water because you've interacted with water), then Swampman's words mean nothing. This reveals that your theory assigns heavy weight to history — is that right?

## Anti-Pattern
Don't treat Swampman as a likely scenario to plan for. It's a diagnostic tool for theories, not a practical concern. If your theory gives absurd results for Swampman, fix the theory.
