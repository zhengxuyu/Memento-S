# The Tree of Life

Every living organism is connected through a single branching tree of descent. Species are not fixed categories but snapshots of evolving lineages. Thinking in terms of the tree forces you to see continuity where intuition sees sharp boundaries — there was no first human, no first mammal, just gradual branching.

## When to Apply
- Classifying entities into categories with fuzzy boundaries
- Reasoning about how current diversity arose from shared origins
- Challenging essentialist thinking (fixed kinds, sharp boundaries)

## Procedure
1. Identify the entities to be related (species, ideas, products, languages)
2. Trace their branching history — when did they diverge from a common ancestor?
3. Note that boundaries between branches are gradual, not sharp
4. Use the tree to explain current similarities (shared ancestry) and differences (divergence)
5. Resist the urge to draw sharp lines between branches — the tree is continuous

## Example
Humans and chimps share ~98.8% of their DNA because they diverged relatively recently (~6 million years ago). The tree explains why: most of the genome was inherited from the common ancestor. The 1.2% difference accumulated after the split.

## Anti-Pattern
Don't treat the tree as a ladder of progress with humans at the top. Every living species is equally "evolved" — they've all been evolving for the same amount of time since the common ancestor.
