# Herb, Alice, and Hal

Dennett uses simple characters — Herb (a regular person), Alice (who claims to have inverted qualia), and Hal (an AI) — to probe our intuitions about consciousness. By systematically varying features of these characters, we discover which assumptions are doing the real work in our thinking.

## When to Apply
- Your intuitions about intelligence or understanding seem strong but unexamined
- You need to isolate which feature of a system drives your judgment
- Debating whether a system "really" has some mental property

## Procedure
1. Construct a simple thought experiment with minimal characters
2. Systematically vary one feature at a time
3. Notice where your intuitions shift — that's where the real assumption lives
4. Ask whether the assumption is justified or just a gut reaction
5. Revise your position based on what the thought experiment reveals

## Example
Does an agent that narrates its reasoning "understand" more than one that acts silently? Construct two identical agents — one verbose, one silent. If you attribute more understanding to the talker, your intuition is tracking verbosity, not comprehension. That's worth knowing.

## Anti-Pattern
Don't over-rely on thought experiments at the expense of empirical evidence. They reveal assumptions but don't settle factual questions.
