# The "Surely" Operator

When someone says "surely X is true," the word "surely" is doing the work that an *argument* should be doing. It signals a claim the speaker believes is obvious but hasn't actually justified. These are exactly the assumptions most worth questioning — if they were truly obvious, they wouldn't need the rhetorical emphasis.

## When to Apply
- You find yourself thinking "obviously..." or "of course..." about a claim
- Reviewing reasoning that contains confidence markers without supporting evidence
- A plan rests on an assumption marked as "clearly true" without testing
- Debugging a failed strategy — look for unexamined "surely" assumptions

## Procedure
1. Scan reasoning for confidence markers: "surely," "obviously," "clearly," "of course," "naturally"
2. For each, extract the underlying claim being asserted without argument
3. Ask: What evidence supports this claim? Has it actually been tested?
4. If untested, flag it as an assumption that needs verification
5. Test the assumption before building further reasoning on it

## Example
Reasoning: "The player obviously needs to reach the green square to win." Flagging "obviously": has this been tested? What if the green square is a decoy, or reaching it is necessary but not sufficient? Remove the "obviously" and treat it as hypothesis H1 to be tested.

## Anti-Pattern
Don't flag every use of "clearly" as a fallacy. Sometimes things genuinely are clear. The tool is for catching *unjustified* confidence, not policing ordinary language.
