# Two Black Boxes

Two black boxes produce identical input-output behavior. One uses a lookup table, the other uses deep reasoning. From the outside, they're indistinguishable. This pumps intuitions about whether internal implementation matters for attributing understanding, consciousness, or competence.

## When to Apply
- Debating whether internal mechanism matters if external behavior is identical
- Evaluating whether a system "really" understands vs. "merely" mimics understanding
- Deciding what level of analysis is appropriate for a given question

## Procedure
1. Specify the two systems and confirm their behavior is identical across all relevant inputs
2. Identify the internal difference (mechanism, architecture, process)
3. Ask: for the property in question (understanding, consciousness), does the internal difference matter?
4. Consider edge cases: would the systems diverge under novel inputs or perturbations?
5. Decide whether your question is about behavior (external) or mechanism (internal)

## Example
Two chatbots give identical responses to every query. One uses a massive lookup table, the other uses a neural network. Does one "understand" more than the other? If your criterion is behavioral, no. If you care about mechanism, maybe. The thought experiment forces you to clarify what you're really asking.

## Anti-Pattern
Don't assume behavioral equivalence is achievable in practice. Real systems almost always diverge under novel conditions, which breaks the thought experiment's premise.
