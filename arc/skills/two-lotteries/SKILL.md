# Two Lotteries

Consider two lotteries: in one, you chose your ticket; in another, a ticket was assigned to you. You win both. The outcomes are equally lucky, but they feel different. This tool distinguishes between types of luck — outcome luck vs. constitutive luck — and clarifies which type matters for evaluation.

## When to Apply
- Evaluating whether an agent's success was skill or luck
- An argument conflates different sources of randomness
- You need to separate "lucky outcome" from "lucky starting conditions"

## Procedure
1. Identify the successful outcome being evaluated
2. Distinguish outcome luck (the dice rolled well) from constitutive luck (the agent happened to have good starting traits)
3. Ask: which type of luck is relevant to the evaluation at hand?
4. Adjust credit or blame accordingly

## Example
An agent scores high on a game because it happened to spawn near a reward (constitutive luck) and then made good decisions from there (skill). Evaluating the agent requires separating these: the spawn was lucky, but the navigation was competent.

## Anti-Pattern
Don't use this to deny all credit by calling everything luck. The point is to distinguish types of luck, not to collapse skill into fortune.
