# Beware of the Prime Mammal

There is no "prime mammal" — no single animal that captures the essence of mammal-hood. Categories in biology (and elsewhere) are populations with variation, not types with essences. Expecting a perfect prototype leads to confused thinking about fuzzy, overlapping categories.

## When to Apply
- You're looking for the "real" or "essential" version of something
- Categories seem to have fuzzy boundaries and you want a clean definition
- You're tempted to ask "but what is X *really*?"

## Procedure
1. Notice when you're seeking the essential or prototypical instance of a category
2. Remind yourself: categories are often population-level patterns, not essences
3. Accept that boundaries may be genuinely fuzzy, not just poorly defined
4. Work with the variation rather than trying to eliminate it
5. Ask what useful work the category does, rather than what it "really means"

## Example
When classifying game objects, you might seek the "true" obstacle — the one thing all obstacles have in common. But obstacles may share family resemblances without a single defining property. Work with the cluster, not a mythical essence.

## Anti-Pattern
Don't conclude that because categories lack essences, they're useless. "Mammal" is a perfectly good category despite having no prime member.
