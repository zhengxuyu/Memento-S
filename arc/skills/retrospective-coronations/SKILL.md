# Widowmakers and Retrospective Coronations

We look back and crown certain moments as "the turning point" or "the key discovery." But at the time, their significance wasn't apparent. This retrospective coronation distorts our understanding of how progress actually happens — it was messier and more contingent than the story suggests.

## When to Apply
- A post-hoc explanation seems too clean or inevitable
- You're identifying "the moment" something changed
- Analyzing why a strategy succeeded or failed after the fact

## Procedure
1. Notice when you're constructing a narrative of "what really happened"
2. Ask: would this event have seemed significant at the time?
3. Consider alternative events that could have been crowned instead
4. Account for luck and contingency in your explanation
5. Prefer explanations that acknowledge messiness over tidy narratives

## Example
After scoring, you trace back and identify "the key move" that led to the score. But at the time, it was one of many exploratory moves. The coronation is retrospective — you only see its importance because you know the outcome.

## Anti-Pattern
Don't refuse to learn from the past. The point is to be honest about what was skill vs. luck, not to abandon retrospective analysis entirely.
