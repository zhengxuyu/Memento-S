# Occam's Broom

Named by Sidney Brenner: the process of selectively ignoring inconvenient facts to make a theory appear simpler and cleaner than it really is. Unlike Occam's Razor (which legitimately favors simplicity), Occam's Broom *manufactures* false simplicity by sweeping away evidence that doesn't fit.

## When to Apply
- A hypothesis looks suspiciously clean — no anomalies, no exceptions
- You notice certain observations are being ignored or downplayed
- Reviewing your own reasoning: am I selectively remembering successes?
- Someone presents a theory that only addresses favorable evidence

## Procedure
1. State the current hypothesis or theory
2. List ALL observations, including ones that don't fit neatly
3. Check: are any observations being ignored, dismissed, or explained away?
4. If yes, those swept-away facts may be the most important ones to investigate
5. Revise the hypothesis to account for the inconvenient evidence

## Example
Hypothesis: "Moving RIGHT always advances the game." Review of all trials shows 3 cases where RIGHT had no effect — but these were ignored because they "seemed like glitches." Occam's Broom detects this: those 3 cases likely reveal a condition (e.g., a wall, a boundary) the hypothesis doesn't account for.

## Anti-Pattern
Don't treat every unexplained observation as equally important. Some noise is genuine noise. The concern is *systematic* omission of inconvenient evidence, not occasional anomalies.
