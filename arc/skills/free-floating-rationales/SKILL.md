# Free-Floating Rationales

There are excellent reasons why trees have deep roots, but no tree or designer ever thought of those reasons. The rationale "floats free" — it exists as an explanatory fact about why something works, without being represented in any mind. Natural selection discovers these rationales without comprehending them.

## When to Apply
- Explaining why evolved structures are well-designed despite having no designer
- Distinguishing between "reasons" as causes and "reasons" as justifications
- Someone insists that design requires a conscious designer with explicit reasons

## Procedure
1. Identify the well-designed feature (adaptation, structure, behavior)
2. State the rationale: why is this design good? What problem does it solve?
3. Check: did any agent explicitly conceive this rationale? (usually no, in biological cases)
4. Explain how the rationale was "discovered" without comprehension (e.g., natural selection)
5. Note that the rationale is real and explanatory even though no mind ever held it

## Example
Birds have hollow bones because it reduces weight for flight. This is an excellent reason — but no bird or bird-ancestor ever thought "I should have hollow bones for weight reduction." The rationale exists as a fact about why hollow bones were selected for, not as anyone's thought.

## Anti-Pattern
Don't conflate free-floating rationales with conscious reasons. When a human engineer designs a bridge, those are represented rationales. The "free-floating" part is specifically about reasons that exist without any mind entertaining them.
