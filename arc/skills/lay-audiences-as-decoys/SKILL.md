# Using Lay Audiences as Decoys

Attempting to explain something to a non-expert forces you to make every step explicit. Gaps in understanding that are invisible when thinking in shorthand become obvious when you must spell out every link in the chain. The "audience" is a decoy — the real beneficiary is you.

## When to Apply
- You feel confident about a plan but can't clearly explain the causal chain
- A strategy "should work" but you're not sure exactly why
- You're stuck and need to find where your reasoning breaks down
- Before committing to a complex multi-step plan

## Procedure
1. Imagine explaining your current understanding to someone with no background
2. Start from the very beginning — state the goal, the known facts, and your plan
3. At each step, ask: "Why does this follow from the previous step?"
4. Where you find yourself saying "it just does" or hand-waving, you've found a gap
5. Fill the gap or flag it as an untested assumption

## Example
Plan: "Move to the green object to score." Explain to a novice: "I move to the green object because... well, green objects have been near scoring events." Gap found: correlation isn't causation. Maybe the green object is incidental and something else caused the score.

## Anti-Pattern
Don't over-simplify to the point where important nuance is lost. The exercise is about making reasoning explicit, not dumbing it down until it becomes wrong.
