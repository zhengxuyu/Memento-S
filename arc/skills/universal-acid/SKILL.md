# Universal Acid

Dennett calls evolution by natural selection a "universal acid" — an idea so powerful it eats through every traditional concept it touches, transforming everything and leaving nothing unchanged. It dissolves the boundary between designed and undesigned, purposeful and purposeless, mind and mechanism.

## When to Apply
- A domain (morality, meaning, consciousness) is assumed to be exempt from evolutionary explanation
- Someone draws a sharp line between "real" design and "mere" natural selection
- You need to test whether an evolutionary/selectionist explanation applies to a new domain

## Procedure
1. Identify the domain or concept assumed to be outside evolutionary reach
2. Ask: could a generate-and-test process (variation + selection) produce this?
3. If yes, trace how the evolutionary process would work in this domain
4. Note what the evolutionary explanation dissolves (designer? purpose? essence?)
5. Assess what remains after the acid does its work — it transforms, not destroys

## Example
Human moral intuitions seem like timeless truths. But universal acid suggests they're products of evolutionary pressures — cooperation, reciprocity, kin selection. This doesn't make morality fake; it reframes its origins. The intuitions are real, but their source is natural selection, not divine decree.

## Anti-Pattern
Don't treat universal acid as a reason for nihilism. Evolution explains origins, not validity. That moral intuitions evolved doesn't mean they're wrong — that's the genetic fallacy.
