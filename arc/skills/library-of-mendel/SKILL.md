# The Library of Mendel

By analogy with Borges' Library of Babel (all possible books), the Library of Mendel contains all possible genomes. Every organism that ever lived or could live has its genome "in" this abstract library. Evolution is a walk through this space, not a creative act — it discovers, not invents.

## When to Apply
- Reasoning about the space of possible designs or solutions
- Explaining why evolution is a search process, not a creative one
- Debating whether evolutionary outcomes are "inevitable" or "contingent"

## Procedure
1. Define the space: what are all possible configurations? (genomes, programs, designs)
2. Recognize that actual instances are a tiny fraction of the possible space
3. Describe the search process: how does the system explore this space? (mutation, recombination)
4. Note that the space exists abstractly — no one needs to enumerate it
5. Use the framework to ask: how much of the space has been explored? What regions remain?

## Example
There are roughly 4^(3 billion) possible human-length genomes. Actual humans occupy a minuscule cluster in this space. Evolution has explored nearby regions through mutation and recombination, but vast territories of possible genomes — and the organisms they'd encode — remain unvisited.

## Anti-Pattern
Don't reify the library — it's a conceptual tool, not a real place. The "existence" of unvisited genomes is abstract, like the existence of unplayed chess games.
