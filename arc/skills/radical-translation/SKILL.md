# Radical Translation

Quine's thought experiment: a linguist encounters a completely unknown language. A native says "gavagai" while pointing at a rabbit. Does it mean "rabbit," "undetached rabbit parts," or "rabbit-event"? All interpretations fit the evidence. Meaning is underdetermined by behavior alone — you need additional constraints (charity, simplicity) to pin it down.

## When to Apply
- Interpreting what an agent or system's signals "mean"
- Multiple interpretations of behavior are equally consistent with observations
- Assigning semantic content to internal representations

## Procedure
1. Observe the behavior or signal to be interpreted
2. Generate at least two distinct interpretations that fit the evidence
3. Apply the principle of charity: prefer the interpretation that makes the agent most rational
4. Apply simplicity: prefer the interpretation that requires fewer ad hoc assumptions
5. Accept that some indeterminacy may be irreducible — not every question about meaning has a fact of the matter

## Example
An AI system outputs "danger" when sensors detect heat. Does it mean "fire," "high temperature," or "sensor-activation-pattern-7"? All are consistent. Charity and context suggest "fire" if the system was designed for fire detection, but the underdetermination is real.

## Anti-Pattern
Don't use indeterminacy as an excuse to assign any interpretation you like. The constraints (charity, simplicity, design history) are real and binding — they just don't always yield a unique answer.
