# Zombies and Zimboes

A "zimbo" is a zombie that monitors itself — it's a philosophical zombie with higher-order representations of its own states. A zimbo would insist it's conscious (just as we do) but supposedly isn't. This pushes the zombie concept to the point of absurdity: if something acts conscious, claims to be conscious, and models its own consciousness, what work is "real consciousness" doing?

## When to Apply
- A thought experiment posits a being that's functionally identical but "really" different
- Someone claims an internal property exists that has zero external manifestation
- Evaluating whether a distinction without observable difference is meaningful

## Procedure
1. Take the proposed zombie and add self-monitoring (making it a zimbo)
2. Note that the zimbo would assert its own consciousness
3. Ask: what evidence could distinguish a zimbo from a conscious being?
4. If no evidence could distinguish them, question whether the distinction is real
5. Apply this test to any claim about undetectable internal properties

## Example
Someone claims an agent "doesn't really understand" despite perfect performance and accurate self-reports about its reasoning. Push back: if it behaves correctly, explains its reasoning correctly, and predicts future behavior correctly — what observable fact would "real understanding" add?

## Anti-Pattern
Don't use this to dismiss all claims about internal states. The point is that distinctions without any possible evidence are suspect, not that inner experience is impossible.
