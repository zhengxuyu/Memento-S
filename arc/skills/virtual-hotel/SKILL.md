# Noise in the Virtual Hotel

In a virtual hotel with many rooms, most activity is noise — random, meaningless fluctuation. The challenge is identifying the rare genuine signals amid the din. Complex systems generate enormous amounts of data, but most of it is uninformative. Learning to filter is essential.

## When to Apply
- Overwhelmed by observations and unsure what's meaningful
- Seeing patterns that might be coincidental
- Trying to extract useful information from a noisy environment

## Procedure
1. Acknowledge that most variation in your observations may be noise
2. Look for patterns that repeat reliably across multiple observations
3. Set a threshold: how many repetitions before you treat a pattern as signal?
4. Test suspected signals by predicting future observations
5. Update your signal/noise classification based on prediction accuracy

## Example
In exploring a game, many objects change position between steps. Most changes are noise (background animation, irrelevant NPCs). The signal is the consistent relationship between your actions and specific state changes. Track correlations across multiple observations before concluding "X causes Y."

## Anti-Pattern
Don't dismiss everything as noise. If you never commit to a pattern being real, you'll never learn anything. Balance skepticism with willingness to act on emerging patterns.
