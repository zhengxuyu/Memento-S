# Vim (Vitality)

"Vim" is the supposed special sauce that makes conscious experience feel alive — the subjective "oomph." Dennett questions whether vim is a real property or just what certain information-processing feels like from the inside. The sense that there must be "something more" may itself be an artifact of the process, not evidence of an extra ingredient.

## When to Apply
- You feel a solution or explanation is missing "something" but can't specify what
- An account seems complete but doesn't "feel" satisfying
- Evaluating whether subjective dissatisfaction indicates a real gap

## Procedure
1. Identify the sense that "something is missing" from an explanation
2. Try to specify exactly what's missing — can you name it?
3. If you can't name it, consider that the feeling itself may be the illusion
4. Ask: does the explanation actually leave out any observable or testable prediction?
5. If all predictions are covered, the "missing something" may be vim — a feeling, not a fact

## Example
Your hypothesis explains all observed score changes, but it "doesn't feel like you understand the game." Ask: what specific prediction fails? If none, the dissatisfaction is vim — a feeling of incompleteness without actual incompleteness. Trust the evidence over the feeling.

## Anti-Pattern
Don't use this to suppress all intuitive dissatisfaction. Sometimes the gut feeling that something's missing is tracking a real gap. Check the evidence first.
