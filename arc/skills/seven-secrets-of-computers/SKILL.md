# The Seven Secrets of Computer Power

Computers achieve extraordinary feats from mundane foundations: (1) arithmetic, (2) conditional branching, (3) addressing/storage, (4) data movement, (5) simple operations composed, (6) speed, and (7) no insight required. The "secret" is that no step requires understanding — intelligence emerges from vast quantities of trivially simple operations.

## When to Apply
- Someone claims a system must "understand" to produce intelligent behavior
- You need to explain how complex outputs arise from mechanical processes
- Debating whether a process requires insight or can be purely algorithmic

## Procedure
1. Break the impressive behavior into its component operations
2. Check: is each operation individually trivial and mechanical?
3. Identify which "secrets" are doing the heavy lifting (usually composition + speed)
4. Demonstrate that no single step requires understanding or insight
5. Show how the combination produces the complex behavior

## Example
A chess engine doesn't understand chess. It evaluates positions using arithmetic, branches on conditions (if score > threshold), stores board states in memory, and does this millions of times per second. Each operation is mindless; the combination plays grandmaster-level chess.

## Anti-Pattern
Don't conclude that because the parts are simple, the whole system is simple or uninteresting. Emergence from simple parts is precisely what needs explaining.
