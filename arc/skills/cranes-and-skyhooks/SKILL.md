# Cranes and Skyhooks

A crane is a legitimate mechanism that builds complexity from the ground up — it's grounded in established foundations and lifts explanatory power incrementally. A skyhook is a miraculous explanation that hangs from nothing — it simply asserts the existence of design, purpose, or complexity without explaining how it got there.

## When to Apply
- Evaluating whether an explanation is grounded or circular
- Someone invokes a designer, vital force, or unexplained leap to explain complexity
- Distinguishing bottom-up explanations from top-down miracles

## Procedure
1. Identify the phenomenon to be explained (complexity, design, purpose)
2. Examine the proposed explanation: does it build from simpler, established components? (crane)
3. Or does it invoke something at least as complex/mysterious as the explanandum? (skyhook)
4. If skyhook, ask: can we replace it with a crane? What ground-up process could produce this?
5. Prefer cranes — they may be less dramatic but they actually explain

## Example
"God designed the eye" is a skyhook — it explains complexity by invoking something more complex. Natural selection is a crane — it builds the eye incrementally from simpler structures, each step grounded in survival advantage. The crane is less intuitive but actually explanatory.

## Anti-Pattern
Don't label every unexplained mechanism a skyhook. Sometimes we know a crane exists but haven't found it yet. "Skyhook" means the explanation is in principle non-mechanistic, not just currently incomplete.
