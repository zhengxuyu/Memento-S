# The Sad Case of Mr. Clapgras

Capgras syndrome patients recognize faces but lack the emotional response — they conclude their loved ones have been replaced by impostors. This reveals that recognition and emotional familiarity are separate systems that normally work together. When they dissociate, bizarre behavior results. Such cases are windows into cognitive architecture.

## When to Apply
- A system partially fails in a way that reveals its hidden components
- Behavior seems bizarre until you realize two normally-coupled systems are decoupled
- Analyzing what cognitive components exist by looking at how they can break apart

## Procedure
1. Identify the bizarre or unexpected behavior
2. Ask: what normally-integrated systems could produce this if decoupled?
3. Hypothesize which component is working and which has failed
4. Test the hypothesis by checking if the working component's outputs are intact
5. Use the dissociation to map the system's architecture

## Example
An agent correctly identifies objects but fails to act on them appropriately — it "sees" a goal but doesn't approach it. This dissociation between perception and action-selection reveals they're separate subsystems. Fix the coupling, not the perception.

## Anti-Pattern
Don't over-interpret every partial failure as revealing deep architecture. Sometimes things just break in simple ways.
