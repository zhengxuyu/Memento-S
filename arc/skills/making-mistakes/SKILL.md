# Making Mistakes

Mistakes are not just tolerable — they are essential for progress. The key is to make mistakes *well*: make them quickly, recognize them clearly, and learn from them systematically. Avoiding all mistakes means avoiding all discovery.

## When to Apply
- An attempted solution has failed and you need to decide how to respond
- Analysis paralysis: too many options, fear of choosing wrong
- Evaluating whether to try a bold or unconventional approach
- After a failure, to extract maximum learning before moving on

## Procedure
1. Acknowledge the mistake explicitly — name what went wrong and why
2. Identify what the mistake *revealed* that you didn't know before
3. Record the lesson: what constraint, assumption, or fact was uncovered?
4. Adjust your model/strategy based on the new information
5. Move forward with the next attempt, incorporating the lesson

## Example
An agent tries moving UP expecting to collect an item, but the score doesn't change. Instead of treating this as pure failure, it notes: "UP near color-1 objects does not score. The item may require a different interaction." This eliminates one hypothesis and narrows the search space.

## Anti-Pattern
Do not treat mistakes as reasons to abandon a line of inquiry entirely. The goal is to fail *informatively*, not to fail and retreat to safe, already-explored territory.
