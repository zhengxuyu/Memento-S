# Deepity

A deepity is a statement with two readings: on one reading it's true but trivial, on another it's profound but false. "Love is just a word" — trivially, yes, the string L-O-V-E is a word; profoundly, no, love is a real phenomenon. Deepities trade on ambiguity to simulate depth.

## When to Apply
- A claim sounds profound but you can't pin down what it actually asserts
- A principle being invoked has a trivial true reading and a deep false reading
- Someone uses poetic or ambiguous language to avoid committing to a testable claim

## Procedure
1. Identify the statement that sounds deep
2. Find the trivially true reading — what interpretation makes it obviously correct?
3. Find the apparently profound reading — what interpretation would be genuinely significant?
4. Check: is the profound reading actually true? If not, the statement is a deepity.

## Example
"Every game is really just about exploration." Trivially true: every game involves some discovery. Profoundly false: many games are primarily about optimization, timing, or resource management. The statement sounds insightful but collapses under scrutiny.

## Anti-Pattern
Don't label every metaphor or generalization as a deepity. Some genuinely deep statements resist easy formalization. A deepity specifically trades on ambiguity between trivial and false readings.
