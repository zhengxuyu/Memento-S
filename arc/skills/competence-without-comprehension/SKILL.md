# Competence Without Comprehension

A termite mound regulates temperature brilliantly, but no termite understands thermodynamics. Natural selection produces exquisite competence — organisms that behave as if they understand — without any comprehension at all. This is the default in nature: comprehension is rare and recent, competence is ancient and ubiquitous.

## When to Apply
- Someone assumes competent behavior requires understanding
- Evaluating whether an AI system needs to "understand" to perform well
- Explaining how evolution produces design without a designer's comprehension

## Procedure
1. Identify the competent behavior (skill, adaptation, capability)
2. Ask: does the system comprehend what it's doing and why?
3. If no comprehension, identify the process that produced the competence (evolution, training, engineering)
4. Note that the competence is real and impressive regardless of comprehension
5. Ask: would adding comprehension improve performance, or is competence sufficient?

## Example
A cuckoo chick pushes host eggs out of the nest — a sophisticated strategy for survival. The chick has zero comprehension of what it's doing or why. Natural selection shaped the behavior because it works, not because any cuckoo understood evolutionary strategy. The competence is real; the comprehension is absent.

## Anti-Pattern
Don't use this to dismiss all understanding as unnecessary. Comprehension, when it exists, enables flexibility, transfer, and novel problem-solving that pure competence lacks. The point is that competence comes first, not that comprehension is worthless.
