# The Qualia Problem

Qualia are supposed to be the irreducibly private, subjective qualities of experience — what red "looks like" or what pain "feels like." Dennett argues that qualia as traditionally defined (private, ineffable, intrinsic, directly apprehensible) don't exist. What exists are dispositional states that we can study functionally.

## When to Apply
- Someone invokes irreducible subjective experience to block analysis
- "You can't understand X without experiencing it" halts investigation
- A functional explanation is dismissed because it "leaves out the experience"

## Procedure
1. Identify the appeal to qualia or irreducible experience
2. Ask: what would change observably if the qualia were different?
3. If nothing observable changes, question whether qualia are doing explanatory work
4. Try to give a functional account of what the qualia-talk is actually tracking
5. If the functional account covers all the data, prefer it over the mysterious version

## Example
Someone says "the agent can't really perceive color 5 the way we do." Ask: what behavioral difference would "really perceiving" it produce? If the agent discriminates, categorizes, and responds to color 5 appropriately, the functional account is sufficient.

## Anti-Pattern
Don't be dismissive of people's experiences. The point is methodological — prefer functional explanations — not that experiences don't exist.
