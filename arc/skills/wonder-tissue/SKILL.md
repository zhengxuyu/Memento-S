# Wonder Tissue

Wonder tissue is Dennett's term for any explanatory move that "explains" a capacity by attributing it to a magical substance that simply has that capacity by fiat. It explains nothing — it just relocates the mystery. If you can't say how the tissue works, you haven't explained anything.

## When to Apply
- An explanation invokes a special substance, module, or force without explaining its mechanism
- Someone says "X does Y because X has the power to do Y"
- A proposed explanation is unfalsifiable because the key component is left as a black box

## Procedure
1. Identify the phenomenon to be explained (e.g., consciousness, understanding)
2. Check: does the proposed explanation invoke something that simply "has" the target property?
3. Ask: how does that component achieve the property? If no answer, it's wonder tissue
4. Demand a mechanistic story — what subprocesses produce the effect?

## Example
"The brain produces consciousness because neurons have a special property called proto-consciousness." This is wonder tissue — you've just pushed the mystery down one level without explaining anything. A real explanation would describe what computational or physical processes give rise to conscious experience.

## Anti-Pattern
Don't dismiss every abstraction as wonder tissue. High-level descriptions are fine as long as you believe a mechanistic story exists underneath, even if you don't know it yet.
