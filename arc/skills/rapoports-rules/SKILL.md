# Rapoport's Rules

Before criticizing or abandoning a position, you must first demonstrate that you understand it at its strongest. This prevents premature dismissal of good ideas and ensures criticism is substantive rather than based on misunderstanding.

## When to Apply
- Evaluating whether to discard a hypothesis that partially worked
- Comparing two competing strategies or explanations
- Reviewing a failed attempt to extract useful elements before moving on
- Any time you're tempted to immediately dismiss an approach

## Procedure
1. Re-express the position so clearly that the holder would say "I wish I'd put it that way"
2. List any points of agreement or partial success
3. State what you have learned from the position
4. Only then may you criticize or reject it

## Example
A failed strategy moved the player to a goal but didn't score. Rapoport's Rules: (1) "The strategy correctly identified the goal location and navigated there efficiently." (2) "The pathfinding worked." (3) "Proximity to the goal alone isn't sufficient for scoring." (4) "The strategy needs an additional interaction step after reaching the goal."

## Anti-Pattern
Don't use steel-manning as an excuse to never reject anything. The point is fair evaluation, not infinite charity. After genuine understanding, firm rejection is appropriate.
