# Murder in Trafalgar Square

Dennett's thought experiment: "There was a murder in Trafalgar Square" could refer to a killing, a flock of crows (a "murder"), or a mystery novel left on a bench. The words are identical but the meaning depends entirely on context and reference. Surface patterns don't determine meaning.

## When to Apply
- The same observation could have multiple interpretations
- A pattern or symbol means different things in different contexts
- You're attributing meaning to a game element based on appearance alone
- Two situations look identical but produce different outcomes

## Procedure
1. Identify the observation or pattern being interpreted
2. List at least three different meanings it could have in context
3. For each interpretation, identify what additional evidence would confirm it
4. Gather that evidence before committing to one interpretation
5. If ambiguity persists, design a test that distinguishes between interpretations

## Example
A color-1 object appears near the player. Interpretations: (1) it's a collectible item, (2) it's a visual indicator of proximity to something, (3) it's a hazard to avoid. Each predicts different outcomes from interaction. Test by approaching vs. avoiding and observing the result.

## Anti-Pattern
Don't use this to claim everything is ambiguous. Often context makes meaning clear. This tool is for cases where you've assumed a meaning without checking whether the context supports that specific interpretation.
