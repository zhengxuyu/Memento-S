# The Self as Center of Narrative Gravity

Just as a center of gravity is a useful mathematical fiction (not a physical atom), the self is a useful narrative fiction — an abstract point around which we organize the story of a person's actions, beliefs, and experiences. It isn't a ghost in the machine.

## When to Apply
- Someone asks "what does the agent really want?" as if there's a single unified answer
- An argument treats the self or agent identity as a concrete, findable thing
- Confusion arises from assuming a single coherent perspective behind complex behavior

## Procedure
1. Identify where "the self" or "the agent" is being treated as a concrete entity
2. Reframe it as a narrative abstraction — a useful summary of many sub-processes
3. Ask what work the abstraction is doing and whether it's pulling its weight
4. Check if the argument still holds once "self" is deflated to its functional role

## Example
A user says "the agent decided to explore instead of exploit." Narrative gravity reminds us this is a convenient summary — internally, multiple heuristics competed and one happened to dominate. There's no homunculus that "decided."

## Anti-Pattern
Don't use this to deny that agents have functional states. The point is that the self is an abstraction, not that it's an illusion with no utility.
