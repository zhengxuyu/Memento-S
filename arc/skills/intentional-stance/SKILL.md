# The Intentional Stance

Dennett's key concept: you can predict a system's behavior at three levels — physical (laws of physics), design (how it was built to function), or intentional (what a rational agent with its information would do). The intentional stance treats any system *as if* it has beliefs and goals, and predicts it will act rationally to achieve those goals.

## When to Apply
- You need to predict behavior but don't know the internal mechanism
- A system is too complex to model mechanistically
- Quick behavioral prediction is more important than deep understanding
- Modeling NPCs, opponents, or any entity whose "goals" are useful abstractions

## Procedure
1. Identify the system whose behavior you want to predict
2. Attribute beliefs: what information does this system have access to?
3. Attribute desires: what goals would make sense given its behavior?
4. Predict: what would a rational agent with those beliefs and desires do?
5. Compare prediction with actual behavior. If mismatched, revise beliefs/desires

## Example
An NPC always moves toward the nearest food item. Intentional stance: "It believes it knows where food is, and it desires food." Prediction: if you place food in a new location, it will redirect toward it. This prediction works without knowing the NPC's actual code.

## Anti-Pattern
Don't confuse the intentional stance with a claim about consciousness. Saying a thermostat "wants" to reach 72 degrees is a predictive strategy, not a claim that thermostats have feelings. The stance is a tool, not a metaphysical position.
