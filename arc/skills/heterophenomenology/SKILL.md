# Heterophenomenology

Take a subject's reports about their experience at face value as sincere testimony — but treat them as data to be explained, not as incorrigible truth. The subject is the authority on what they believe they experience, but not necessarily on what is actually happening internally.

## When to Apply
- An agent or user reports on its own internal state ("I'm confused," "I see a pattern")
- You need to interpret self-reports without either dismissing or blindly trusting them
- Debugging requires distinguishing what a system reports from what it actually does

## Procedure
1. Record the self-report faithfully — what does the subject say they experience?
2. Treat this as the subject's "heterophenomenological world" — their believed experience
3. Look for independent evidence that corroborates or contradicts the report
4. Explain the report (whether accurate or mistaken) without privileging it over behavior

## Example
An agent reports "I'm stuck in a loop." Heterophenomenology says: log that report, then independently check action history. Maybe it is looping, or maybe it tried two similar-looking but distinct strategies and mislabeled them.

## Anti-Pattern
Don't dismiss self-reports as worthless, and don't treat them as gospel. Both extremes miss the point.
