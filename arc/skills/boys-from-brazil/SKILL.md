# The Boys from Brazil

In Levin's novel, cloning Hitler and recreating his childhood doesn't produce another Hitler — because genes and initial conditions massively underdetermine outcomes. Environment, contingency, and developmental noise all matter. This applies to any system where initial parameters are assumed to be destiny.

## When to Apply
- Someone assumes identical initialization produces identical behavior
- An argument treats nature (parameters) as the sole determinant, ignoring nurture (environment)
- Cloning a successful agent's weights is expected to replicate its performance exactly

## Procedure
1. Identify the assumption that initial conditions determine outcomes
2. List the environmental and contingent factors that also shape the outcome
3. Note how small differences in environment can produce large differences in result
4. Conclude that initial conditions constrain but don't determine the outcome

## Example
An agent performs well on game X. You clone its weights for game Y, expecting similar performance. Boys from Brazil: the weights were shaped by game X's specific environment. Game Y's different dynamics will interact with those weights unpredictably.

## Anti-Pattern
Don't conclude that initial conditions don't matter at all. They constrain outcomes — the point is they don't fully determine them.
