# Occam's Razor

Do not multiply entities beyond necessity. When two hypotheses explain the same evidence equally well, prefer the simpler one. Simpler theories are easier to test, more likely to generalize, and less likely to be overfitted to noise.

## When to Apply
- Multiple hypotheses explain the current observations
- A theory requires increasingly complex special cases to work
- You're unsure which hypothesis to test first
- An explanation involves hidden variables, coincidences, or elaborate mechanisms

## Procedure
1. List competing hypotheses that explain the observed evidence
2. For each, count the independent assumptions required
3. Check that simpler hypotheses actually account for all observations (not just most)
4. Prefer and test the simplest sufficient hypothesis first
5. Only add complexity when simpler explanations fail against evidence

## Example
Observation: the player's score increases after a specific sequence of moves. Complex hypothesis: "a hidden timer triggers scoring at intervals." Simple hypothesis: "the final move in the sequence causes the score." Test the simple one first — just try the final move alone.

## Anti-Pattern
Don't use Occam's Razor to reject a complex explanation that fits the evidence better. It's a tie-breaker for equally explanatory hypotheses, not a mandate to always pick the simplest story regardless of fit.
