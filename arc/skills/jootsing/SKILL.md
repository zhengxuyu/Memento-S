# JOOTSING (Jumping Out Of The System)

Coined by Douglas Hofstadter: creative breakthroughs come from knowing a system's rules deeply enough to know *which ones to break*. You can't meaningfully break rules you don't understand. First master the system, then deliberately violate its constraints to discover new possibilities.

## When to Apply
- All conventional approaches have been tried and failed
- The problem feels impossible within current assumptions
- You suspect the constraints themselves may be wrong or incomplete
- You need a creative leap rather than incremental progress

## Procedure
1. Explicitly list the rules/constraints/assumptions of the current system
2. Verify you understand *why* each rule exists (or is assumed to exist)
3. Identify which rules are hard constraints vs. soft assumptions
4. Deliberately violate one soft assumption and observe what happens
5. If the violation reveals new possibilities, explore that direction

## Example
Assumed rule: "The player must reach the goal object to score." After exhausting movement-based approaches, JOOTS: what if the player needs to move *away* from the goal? Testing the opposite assumption reveals that the goal follows the player, and scoring requires a different interaction entirely.

## Anti-Pattern
Don't JOOTS prematurely. Breaking rules you haven't mastered produces random flailing, not creative insight. First exhaust conventional approaches and genuinely understand the system.
