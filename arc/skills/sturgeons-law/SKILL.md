# Sturgeon's Law

"90% of everything is crud." When evaluating any category — approaches, hypotheses, outputs — most instances will be mediocre or bad. The valid question is whether the *best* 10% is any good, not whether the average is.

## When to Apply
- Tempted to dismiss an entire class of strategies because most attempts failed
- Evaluating a large set of options and need to focus attention
- Someone argues "X never works" based on typical examples rather than best examples
- Filtering signal from noise in observations or experimental results

## Procedure
1. Identify the category being evaluated
2. Acknowledge that most instances will be low-quality — this is normal
3. Actively seek out the best examples or attempts within the category
4. Evaluate the category based on its best representatives
5. Decide based on the potential of the best, not the average

## Example
After 10 attempts to score, 9 failed completely but 1 yielded a point. Sturgeon's Law says: don't conclude "this approach doesn't work." Instead, analyze the 1 success — what made it different? That's where the signal is.

## Anti-Pattern
Don't use this to excuse persistent failure. If the best 10% is also crud, the category genuinely has a problem. Sturgeon's Law is about fair evaluation, not wishful thinking.
