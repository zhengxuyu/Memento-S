# The Wandering Self

You are not the same person you were at age five — your cells, beliefs, and personality have all changed. Yet you persist as "you" through continuous, gradual change. Identity is a process, not a snapshot. This applies to any system that evolves over time while maintaining continuity.

## When to Apply
- Someone argues an agent "isn't the same agent" after updates or learning
- Identity is treated as requiring a fixed, unchanging core
- Evaluating whether a continuously learning system maintains meaningful continuity

## Procedure
1. Identify the claim that change breaks identity
2. Note the continuous chain linking the earlier and later states
3. Ask: is there a principled point where identity "broke," or was change gradual?
4. Reframe identity as a continuous process rather than a fixed essence

## Example
After 100 episodes of learning, an agent behaves very differently from its initial state. Someone says "it's a completely different agent now." The wandering self: if the change was gradual and each step preserved continuity, it's the same agent — evolved, not replaced.

## Anti-Pattern
Don't use this to claim any amount of change preserves identity. Sudden, total replacement (like overwriting all weights) is a different case from gradual learning.
